The best game for your group size — 2, 4, 6 or 8 players

November 27, 2026

Every Muddy's Games game lets up to 8 people join one room. But the “most fun” game changes a little depending on how many of you there are. We asked the creator for a pick for each group size.

2 players: Hit & Blow (or Labyrinth .VS)

For two players, the creator recommends Hit & Blow and Labyrinth .VS. Both are one-on-one games where you can take your time and really battle it out.

If it is truly just the two of you, go with Hit & Blow. It is a one-on-one number-guessing game: you find your opponent’s secret number using “Hit” and “Blow” hints. You can choose 3 or 4 digits, so start with 3 digits, then move up to 4 for a good brain workout.

Labyrinth .VS was the very first Muddy's Games game. The creator still says “it’s just fun” — it is the game closest to their heart.

4 players: CUT & CLAIM

For four players, try CUT & CLAIM. You draw lines to enclose areas and grow your own territory.

It works with 8 players too, but with 8 the field gets crowded and messy. The rule for taking back territory that someone took from you also seems a little hard for first-timers. With about four players, you can see who owns what, and the back-and-forth of taking and retaking land is just right. If you are short on players, you can add CPUs.

Party-Arcade is plenty of fun with four, and Labyrinth .VS doubles (two teams of two) is another great way to play.

6 players: Party-Arcade

For six players, Party-Arcade. It is a party game where you compete for points across 15 mini-games. Reflexes, timing, memory — a different person shines in each game, so the more players you have, the more the ranking shuffles, and nobody knows who will win until the end.

Of course, it is a blast with 8 players too. Six or eight, it is fun either way.

8 players: Drawing Telephone

When all eight of you are together, play Drawing Telephone. The prompt is passed from person to person using drawings only, so the more players there are, the more the picture changes along the way.

Once, when the creator played a similar app years ago, the prompt was “candle”. Somewhere along the line it turned into “cake”, and by the end it had become “pudding”. Watching everyone’s drawings in order at the end shows exactly where it changed — and the whole group bursts out laughing.

Not sure? Labyrinth .VS

When most of the group is new, the creator’s pick for the very first game is Labyrinth .VS. The rules might feel a little tricky at first, but what you actually do is simple: tap a square to move. When you hit an invisible wall, it is your opponent’s turn.

There is also a computer mode, so if you play once on your own first, explaining it to everyone becomes much easier.
